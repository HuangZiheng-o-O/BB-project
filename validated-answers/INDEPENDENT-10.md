# Question

Does missing the January 8 individual appointment establish that Rowan stopped participating in treatment? What later evidence bears on this?

# Answer

No. The January 8 appointment was a **no-show**: Rowan was not seen for that individual visit. Later that day, Rowan returned an outreach call and said they intended to attend the next day’s family appointment; that call addressed scheduling, not therapy. [BH-D015:L9,L11,L13,L15,L17]

Later records show continued participation, not a stop in treatment. Rowan attended and engaged in family therapy on January 9, contributed to group on January 12, and completed individual therapy on January 14. At the January 14 visit, Rowan and the clinician discussed the missed appointment as an attendance barrier rather than a reason to abandon the schedule. [BH-D007:L6,L7,L9,L16] [BH-D009:L14,L16] [BH-D011:L6,L7,L15] Rowan also participated in an individual contact on January 19 and, later in the month, attended group on January 29 and joined family therapy on January 30. [BH-D105:L5,L6,L9,L11] [BH-D108:L12,L20] [BH-D113:L7,L11]

Participation was not uninterrupted: Rowan also missed a January 27 group and cancelled a January 28 individual appointment. Those later absences matter, but they do not turn the January 8 no-show into evidence that Rowan stopped participating in treatment. [BH-D108:L16,L18]
