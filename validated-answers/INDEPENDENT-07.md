# Question

How did Rowan and Casey's planned support pattern develop from the January 9 family visit through the January 30 family visit? Which observations suggest benefit, and what longer-term outcome is not established?

# Answer

From the January 9 family visit, Rowan and Casey moved toward **predictable, choice-based support**. Rowan said repeated questions about contacting work felt like being watched, while Casey worried that giving space might leave Rowan isolated. They practiced stating and reflecting specific requests. Casey agreed to one planned check-in instead of repeated reminders; Rowan agreed to say whether they wanted practical help or quiet company. They also chose an evening walk as time together that did not have to become a progress review. [BH-D007:L12,L14,L16,L18] [BH-D008:L11,L13,L15]

The early reports suggest some benefit, but not a settled pattern. On January 14, Rowan said the planned check-in had reduced repeated reminders and described opening a work message and taking short walks, though they had not replied and still found mornings and sleep difficult. [BH-D011:L11] In a **Casey-only collateral contact** on January 16—not a visit with Rowan—Casey reported fewer unplanned reminders and less argument when asking what kind of help Rowan wanted. Casey also reported that work conversations could still make Rowan quiet; the plan was to keep offering a walk or meal without taking ownership of Rowan’s tasks. [BH-D012:L8,L11,L13,L15,L17]

At the January 30 family visit, the pair identified a remaining cycle: a work reminder could become a long discussion, after which Rowan withdrew from the task. They rehearsed Casey asking first whether Rowan wanted company, practical help, or a later check-in, and Rowan naming both a specific request and when a reminder felt overwhelming. Both agreed again to **one brief check-in at a planned time** rather than repeated questions. Rowan said that limited plan felt more manageable, while remaining anxious about the work conversation. [BH-D113:L9,L11,L13]

These observations support **short-term improvement in communication and tolerability of support**, not a demonstrated long-term outcome. The January 30 review noted initial work-related steps, including a sent message, but continued delayed follow-up, avoidance, sleep disruption, and functional impact. The record does not establish that the new support pattern persisted or that Rowan completed a return to work. [BH-D115:L8,L10,L12]
