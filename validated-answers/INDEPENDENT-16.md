# Question

For the Monday–Sunday week starting January 26, how does the disputed January 26 start time affect the 150-minute goal? State what is certain and what remains undecided.

# Answer

For the Monday–Sunday week **January 26–February 1**, the **three-therapy-day requirement is met**, but the **150-minute requirement is undecided**. The treatment plan requires at least three therapy days *and* 150 minutes of patient-present therapy [BH-D003:L12].

The dispute concerns **one January 26 encounter, not two sessions**. Its primary final record says patient contact ran **09:00–09:50 (50 minutes)**; the participating clinician’s final record says Rowan entered at **09:10** and contact ran **09:10–09:50 (40 minutes)** [BH-D110:L3,L7,L15,L16] [BH-D111:L4,L7,L10,L16,L17]. Neither record establishes which start time should supersede the other.

The other countable time is **75 minutes** in the January 29 group (90 scheduled minutes less a 15-minute nontherapeutic break, with attendance throughout) and **30 patient-present minutes** in the January 30 family session; its first 15 minutes were partner-only [BH-D107:L12,L13,L17] [BH-D108:L12,L20] [BH-D113:L5-L7]. The January 27 no-show and January 28 cancellation add no treatment time [BH-D108:L10,L11,L16,L18].

Thus the week totals **40 + 75 + 30 = 145 minutes** if the 09:10 start is correct—**5 short**—or **50 + 75 + 30 = 155 minutes** if the 09:00 start is correct—**5 over**. January 26, 29, and 30 establish the three therapy days either way. Resolving whether the 150-minute goal was met requires evidence that settles the conflicting January 26 patient-contact start time.
